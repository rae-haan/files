Likely Operational Approach
At this point, our assessment is that the Group will operate on a shorter timeline with less concern for long-term persistence, favouring an opportunistic and noisy approach. Pre-election activity is expected to focus on reconnaissance against public-facing web infrastructure, credential harvesting through exposed databases or phishing, preparation of DDoS infrastructure, and dry-run probes against target systems. On election day, we anticipate peak activity — DDoS timed to coincide with voting or results reporting, defacement of results pages, and release of any exfiltrated data. Post-election, activity may continue through the release of selectively edited or fabricated internal communications to undermine confidence in the declared result.
General Indicators of Compromise
Supporting this assessment, defenders should be alert to volumetric DoS traffic targeting web infrastructure timed around key election milestones, and credential stuffing attempts against publicly accessible portals such as webmail and CMS admin panels using previously breached databases. Defacement or unauthorised content changes on public-facing electoral websites should be treated as confirmed compromise. Social engineering attempts targeting IT staff or electoral workers — particularly calls impersonating technical support or government agencies — are consistent with this Group's methods. Evidence of unusual outbound data transfers from council systems, and any communications on underground forums advertising access to or data from the target municipality, should also be treated as high-priority indicators.
